# San Marco — Public life atlas v0.1

## Analytical proposition
The same monumental ensemble accommodates through-movement, free lingering, paid café occupation, ceremonies and exceptional event crowd management. This is a hypothesis to test through dated evidence and mapped spatial footprints, not a completed behavior survey.

## Layers to map on a common, calibrated plan
| Layer | What to draw | Primary evidence | Required caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Movement | entrances, paths from Mercerie, Calle Larga XXII Marzo and waterfront; crossings Piazza/Piazzetta | mapped public streets and dated on-site observation | network potential ≠ observed footfall |
| S1 Free staying | sitting, standing, photographing, meeting, shade-seeking | time-stamped observation/photo series | avoid extrapolating a single photograph |
| C1 Café occupation | Florian/Quadri outdoor tables, service routes, public circulation clearance | municipal public-ground concessions and measured footprints | café tables are not freely available public seating |
| E1 Event conversion | stage, crowd sectors, barriers, emergency access, backstage, queues | official event layouts and dated event documents | each event has a different temporary layout |
| T1 Time | morning/day/evening; weekday/weekend; peak/low season | repeat observations | no invented occupancy counts |

## Documented regulatory and event evidence
- Venice Municipality approved a revised **public-area commerce plan for Area Marciana and former Royal Gardens** by Giunta resolution 28 of **29 February 2024**, with a PDF plan and market schedule. Source: https://www.comune.venezia.it/it/content/revisione-piano-il-commercio-aree-pubbliche-area-marciana-e-giardinetti-ex-real . **This is about public-area commerce, not automatically a measured plan of all café terraces.**
- Municipal rules on commercial activities in Venice's historic center include provisions to protect the Piazza San Marco heritage area: https://www.comune.venezia.it/it/node/33517 . Specific terrace concession extents remain to be acquired.
- A documented **2012 Carnival event** in Piazza San Marco used a stage, a public free-admission area and separate backstage/VIP space: https://muoversi.venezia.it/en/content/case-historieshard-rock-cafe . This is evidence for one historical event configuration, not a permanent arrangement.
- Venice official heritage guidance notes public-space rules and seasonal stewards in the Piazza: https://www.veneziaunica.it/en/sustainable-venice/piazza-san-marco-the-monumental-heart .

## Core design questions
1. How much ground is free to occupy without payment versus commercially licensed? Measure only after obtaining concessions and a calibrated base map.
2. Does the open center function primarily as a ceremonial void while everyday occupation clusters at edges? Test by repeated observation, not photographs alone.
3. How do porticoes create a weather-protected transition between public movement and paid occupation?
4. What changes between ordinary day and Carnival/event day, and what infrastructure makes temporary conversion possible?
5. How do tourist flows affect residents' everyday use? Distinguish hypotheses from evidenced observations.

## Field-observation protocol
Record date, hour, weather, season, event status, observer/viewpoint, 10–15 minute sampling window, movement directions, approximate group sizes, free versus paid sitting, temporary barriers, source image ID, and uncertainty. Repeat at comparable time windows; comply with privacy and local photography rules.

## Deliverable
Four synchronized annotated maps (movement, staying, commerce, event) + two source-grounded sections + photo/viewpoint register + limitations. Space syntax is deliberately deferred until the base mapping and behavior evidence are validated.
