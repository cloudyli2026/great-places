# San Marco — historical event plan (1781) and drawing source audit

Research date: 2026-11-07.

## A newly identified original historical plan

**Antonio Codognato, _Planimetria di Piazza S. Marco_, 1781.** Catalogued by CIRICE, Università degli Studi di Napoli Federico II, item Rif.CD 2418: https://www.iconografiacittaeuropea.unina.it/cms/planimetria-di-piazza-s-marco/

The catalog states that the plan shows the **temporary arena for festivities honoring the Dukes of the North**, with the Piazza paving designed by Andrea Tirali visible beneath it. This is an unusually useful primary historical source for studying temporary occupation of the monumental ground, rather than merely the static square.

**Verification status:** identity, date, author and depicted temporary arena are supported by the institutional catalog. The catalog's *Scala* field is empty: **no verified scale**. No dimensions, area, seating capacity, entrance count, or crowd flow should be inferred without viewing a high-resolution plate and independent corroboration. Copyright/reuse permissions for the reproduction remain to be checked.

### Interpretation protocol
1. Preserve the historical plan as a dated **event layer**, separate from present-day geometry.
2. Transcribe the arena outline, temporary structures, openings and Piazza paving only where actually legible.
3. If a modern georeferenced plan is later available, align the historical plate through explicitly documented common control points, recording uncertainty; do not assume the old plan is metrically accurate.
4. Compare with **one dated contemporary festival plan** to test how different governance and crowd-management systems appropriate the same public ground.
5. Keep historical interpretation separate from present-day use claims.

**Status:** file names and existence are confirmed by the public index; the PDFs' drawing contents, graphic scale, annotation and vertical datum have **not** been independently inspected in this research iteration. No building height or portico clearance is extracted yet. The architect's drawing is a source for the Procuratie Vecchie intervention, not automatically a complete measured section across the Piazza.

## An additional historic scaled plan lead

Penn State University Libraries' collection holds a Piazza San Marco plan reproduced from Banister Fletcher, _A History of Architecture on the Comparative Method_ (6th ed., 1921), p.105. Catalog description explicitly says **'a measurement scale'** is present, and labels it public domain: https://www.flickr.com/photos/psulibscollections/5835862325

**Status:** published historical diagram with scale; scale divisions have **not yet been visually checked**, and the 385×467 pixel online image may be too low-resolution for accurate digitization. Do not treat it as a present-day survey.
